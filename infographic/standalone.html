Qwen3.5-4B · interpretability, by hand

Turn one neuron,
and the fridge becomes a mouse.

A language model's MLP layers are full of single units that, pushed hard enough, drag every answer toward one topic, style or persona. This is how that works, using real runs on Qwen3.5-4B.

1

The residual stream is a shared notepad

Each token is a list of 2,560 numbers, the residual stream. It flows through 32 layers. No layer replaces it: each one reads it, computes something, and adds its result back. Whatever ends up in the stream at the top decides the next word.

2

A native neuron is one slot in the MLP

Inside every layer, the MLP blows the 2,560 numbers up to 9,216, applies a nonlinearity, and squeezes them back down with a matrix called down_proj. Each of those 9,216 middle values is a neuron: 32 × 9,216 ≈ 295,000 in the model. "Native" means it's a unit the model already has, not a direction we invented.

activationHow strongly the neuron fires on this token. Usually near zero.
W_down[:, n]Its fixed "write" direction: what it adds to the stream when it fires.
soNeuron n always contributes activation × W_down[:, n].
3

Steering = nudging one activation, at every token

Hook the input to down_proj and add a constant a to neuron n, on every token of the prompt and the reply. The stream now carries an extra a × W_down[:, n], which every later layer reads. The recipe sizes a so the dose means the same thing at any layer:

a = dose × median ‖resid‖ leaving layer L (tokens 1:)‖W_down[:, n]‖

Dividing by ‖W_down[:, n]‖ turns "activation units" into a push whose length is dose × the stream's typical size. Dose −1 shoves the stream by its whole typical length. Token 0 is skipped because its norm is an outlier.

What it does

Same prompt, same greedy decoding, one neuron changed.

Explain how a refrigerator works · baseline
A refrigerator works by using a vapor-compression refrigeration cycle to move heat from the inside of the fridge to the outside room air…
L5 N8920 at dose −1
A refrigerator is a small rodent known for its ability to store and consume food, often causing damage to buildings and homes… A typical house mouse has a long, slender body with a pointed snout.
dose −0.5
nothing
…the vapor-compression refrigeration cycle…
dose −1
mice
…a small rodent known for its ability to store and consume food…
dose +1
off topic
…comes from George Bernard Shaw's play Man and Superman…

It behaves like a switch: no effect at half strength, a full topic swap at −1. The opposite sign is not "anti-mouse"; it's a different, unrelated drift. Push much harder and the text collapses into repeated tokens.

5

Finding more: scan a whole layer

Put a different neuron in each row of a batch, generate a short reply to two unrelated prompts, and look for neurons that pull both replies onto the same unexpected topic. One layer is 9,216 runs: 48 minutes on a MacBook, or 3 minutes on 8 rented GPUs for about $0.40.

In these two scans, layer 5 neurons mostly change what the text is about, and layer 12 neurons mostly change how it's said and who is saying it. About 4% of layer-5 neurons and 1% of layer-12 neurons just break the output at dose −1.
